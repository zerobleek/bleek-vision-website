THE STUDIO

One Dev. One Laptop. One Idea at a time.

Growing up in NYC in the '80s and '90s, three things defined cool: video games, Michael Jackson, and Japan. My first real gaming memory is Super Mario Bros. on my cousin's NES. I dragged my mom into the room to watch, then begged her for one that Christmas. She came through. A love story began.

In 1995, Sony released the PlayStation. Another cousin had one, and we spent an entire holiday break in front of it, surviving on soda and snacks. The futuristic grey shell, the CD discs, the 3D worlds: mind-blowing. It wasn't just a new console. It was proof that games weren't a hobby. They were a way of life. Since then, I've watched gaming grow from something kids did after school into the world's biggest form of entertainment, enjoyed by kids and adults alike.

Professionally, I've spent 16 years as a paralegal in commercial real estate, working acquisitions and dispositions: concurrent deals, hard deadlines, and documents where one missed detail costs real money. That work taught me to break a complex problem into clear steps. It also showed me, up close, where the tools people depend on fall short. All the while, I watched technology reshape everything around me, and I studied the people driving it: CEOs like Steve Jobs and Satya Nadella, and engineers like Mark Cerny. But what finally pushed me to build was the stories of independent developers who turned one idea into one app with real impact. I learned by shipping projects, with AI as my senior dev, and I took the leap.

Bleek Vision LLC is where that story continues. Five apps shipped across three categories, a fitness RPG in active development, and a web platform already in the field, all built with AI as a partner in the work. Whenever the format allows, there's a story underneath: a running app told as a spy thriller, a weather app that talks like the city it covers, a puzzle game organized into chapters. That's no accident. It's the kid on the floor with a controller, still asking what makes something worth coming back to.

I don't plan to stay in one lane. I plan to own every lane I step into.

— Shabaka Malik Banks, Founder & CEO, Bleek Vision LLC
